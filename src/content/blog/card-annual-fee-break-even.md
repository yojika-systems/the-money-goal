## Compare extra rewards, not total rewards
If a fee card earns rewards you could also earn on a no-fee alternative, those shared rewards do not pay for the fee. Compare the difference on the same eligible spending. Subtract the annual fee and any other costs you want the comparison to include.

The threshold comes from dividing fixed costs by the difference in reward rates. That shortcut needs a positive rate difference and enough uncapped eligible spending to reach it.

## Caps can reverse the answer
A reward cap stops one side of the comparison from increasing while alternative rewards may continue. The first break-even point is therefore not a promise that every larger spending amount improves the result. Excluded categories reduce eligible spending too.

## Include the cost of borrowing
Enter interest and other fees separately. A simple reward comparison does not calculate interest from a card balance. The [CFPB explains](#source-cfpb-interest) that transaction types and calculation conventions can differ, so use the relevant account documents.

## Use redeemable value
A headline points rate is not automatically the value you can use. The calculator assumes entered reward rates express redeemable money value and excludes welcome bonuses, tax and non-cash perks. Check expiry, redemption restrictions and current provider terms before comparing actual products.
