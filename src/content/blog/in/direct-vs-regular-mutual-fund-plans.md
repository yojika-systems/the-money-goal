## Distinguish the plan from the portfolio
Direct and regular versions belong to the same mutual fund scheme. Their underlying portfolio is shared, but distribution-related expenses differ. The [AMFI guide](#source-amfi-direct) explains how the direct route excludes distributor commission.

## Understand what the route provides
An investor using a direct route takes responsibility for scheme selection and execution, or separately obtains appropriate advice. A regular route involves a distributor. A lower published expense ratio describes a cost; it does not establish that the scheme fits a person's risk or needs.

## Check the purchase channel
Direct plans can be accessed through fund websites and other documented channels. A demat account is not a requirement for all direct-plan purchases. Check the actual plan label and platform terms rather than assuming every investing interface offers the same option.

## Compare costs using matching assumptions
The [fee comparison example](/blog/expense-ratio-cost-over-25-years) holds contributions and gross returns constant. That isolates a hypothetical expense difference. Real funds have variable returns and changing costs, so an illustration is not a forecast.

Read current scheme documents for risks and charges. This explanation does not recommend a fund, appoint a distributor or offer personalised investment advice.
