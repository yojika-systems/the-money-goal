## Separate the surprise from the calendar
An emergency reserve is for unplanned costs. An annual bill you know is coming belongs in a separate cash-flow plan. Mixing the two can make a reserve look larger than the amount actually available for a shock.

## Build scenarios before choosing a target
List essential spending and consider what changes if income stops, an appliance fails or a dependant needs help. Ask which costs continue, what other support is dependable, and how quickly money would be needed. A single universal target cannot answer those questions for every household.

## Access and risk are part of the decision
The [CFPB guide](#source-cfpb-emergency) discusses keeping emergency money safe and accessible. A product labelled liquid is not automatically a protected bank deposit. Withdrawal restrictions, potential losses and protection depend on the product and country.

## Make the plan practical
A repeatable contribution can build a reserve gradually. After using it for a qualifying expense, review what needs replenishing. Define the purpose in advance so an ordinary purchase does not quietly consume the buffer.

The [financial health check](/financial-health) provides prompts for reviewing cash flow and resilience. It does not inspect your accounts or prescribe a savings product.
