## Start with the account documents
An annual percentage rate describes a yearly rate, but the method used to apply it affects the charge on a statement. The [CFPB explanation](#source-cfpb-interest) describes how card issuers can use daily balances and transaction-specific rates.

## Separate transaction categories
Purchases and other transactions may have different rates or conditions. A single blended rate in a calculator is therefore a simplification. Check which balance a payment is applied to and which rate applies to the remaining amount.

## Know what a monthly model does
Our [debt repayment calculator](/calculators/debt-payoff) uses a monthly rate derived from entered APR, fixed minimum payments and no new transactions. It compares two strategies under the same assumptions. It does not recreate a lender's daily ledger or automatically update promotional terms.

## Check a result before using it
If your statement and a model differ, compare balance dates, transaction categories, fees and the payment convention. Do not change a real payment based only on a simplified estimate. Account-specific questions belong with the issuer or an appropriately qualified adviser.
